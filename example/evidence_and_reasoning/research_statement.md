## The question

*Market days in a synthetic market roll* asks: does the market roll of a
fictional town for 1851 and 1852, a synthetic file made for this
project, support what a fictional note, made for the project beside the
roll, states of the market days of 1851: their count, and the days of the
week they fell on? A count of the roll's rows for 1851, by a check that
would fail were the count otherwise, set against the note's figure as
collated against the note itself, answers the first; the weekday of each
row of 1851, set against what the note says of them, answers the second.
A count reported from memory, or the note's figure quoted without
collation, does not.

## The work this project does

1. **Establish the base.** Count the roll's rows for 1851 by a program
   that would fail were the count otherwise, and collate the note's
   stated figure against the note (`MANIFESTO.md` §5).
2. **Classify the inputs.** For the count, record what is derived (the
   dates the roll gives), what is imposed (the calendar year as the
   year's boundary, a choice of this project, and a row of the roll,
   whatever its kind, as a market day held, the rule of reading), and
   what is conjectured
   (nothing at inception) (`CHECK_METHODOLOGY.md` §4).
3. **Pursue what the base raises.** Where the two figures differ, decide
   what the roll itself can decide about the difference, and set aside to
   a plan of its own what it cannot.
4. **Read the weekdays.** Take the weekday of each row of 1851, its date
   read on the Gregorian calendar (imposed: a choice of this project), and
   set the weekdays against what the note says of them.

## Scope and what is not claimed

- No new result is claimed at inception.
- Material from another repository, a private collection, or unpublished
  work is imported and re-verified before anything builds on it
  (`MANIFESTO.md` §6).
- The sources are synthetic: the project claims nothing about any real
  town, roll, or note, and its results are results about the two files
  it holds.

## Method

Per `MANIFESTO.md` and `CHECK_METHODOLOGY.md`. The unit of verification is
the check; a negative result is a result; no claim is worded stronger than
its evidence.
