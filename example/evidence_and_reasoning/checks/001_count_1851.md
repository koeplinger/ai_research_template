# Check 001: the roll's count of market days in 1851

*Created 20 November 2026; updated 20 November 2026.*
Plan: 001, task 1
Backs: [claim 001]
Instrument: the held roll at its recorded checksum, read with the Python standard library, CPython 3.12.3
Mutation: shift-year, drop-month, duplicate-entry, relabel-kind

By: assistant, model withheld from this shipped example

## 1. What is checked

That the roll [Roll1851] holds 51 rows dated 1851, of which 49 are
ordinary days and 2 are fairs, on 51 distinct days; that every row
counted is dated 1851; and that no row dated 1851 is left out. This is
the count [claim 001] states. The rule of reading, that a row of 1851 is
a market day whatever its kind, was written down before this check read
the roll, in `evidence_and_reasoning/notes/2026-11-20_counting_protocol.md`;
no commit evidences that order, the project being committed once at its
end. The roll was made by a program whose rule is recorded with it
(`prompt_logs/005_sources.md`), so the protocol fixes the rule of
reading and not the outcome.

## 2. Construction

The program `python_project/src/check_001_count_1851.py` first confirms
that the roll it reads is the held file whose SHA-256 checksum
`source_documents/Roll1851_market_roll_sha256.txt` lists, and prints the
interpreter it runs under; a roll that is not the held file is not parsed.
It then reads the roll through the project's reader,
`python_project/src/roll.py`, as (date, kind) pairs in file order; the
reader refuses a line that is not a row of three fields, a blank line
included, rather than skipping it, and its refusals are tested in
`python_project/tests/test_roll.py`; a quoted field left open is the one
case it does not refuse, the held roll having no quotation mark, and the
second route below would show a line of 1851 lost. A row
belongs to 1851 when its date falls in the calendar year 1851, the
year's boundary (`evidence_and_reasoning/terminology.md`), which the
reader's `in_year` fixes. The count is the number of such rows; the
kinds are the roll's own, ordinary and fair; the days are the distinct
dates among them.

A second route, independent of the reader: the held file's lines,
read as text, are counted month by month by the first eight characters
of their date, and must agree with the reader's count in every month and
in all. A row added, dropped, or dated into another month shows there as
a disagreement in its month; a kind misread is caught by the count of
each kind instead.

## 3. Sanity checks

Named in the program and reported by it, each with a control that must
fail: the roll is the held file its checksum names (bytes the list does
not name fail the comparison); every row is dated 1851 or 1852 (a
planted row dated 31 December 1850 fails it, and one dated 1 January
1853); every kind is ordinary or fair (a planted row of another kind
fails it, and the reader refuses one outright, as its tests show, with a
date it cannot read); the rows are in date order (two rows swapped fail
it). The count's own controls run through the same functions: one row
planted in 1851 makes it 52; a fair relabeled ordinary splits it 50 and
1; a date held twice gives fewer days than rows; and the first row of
1851, dropped from the reader's rows, makes its month disagree with the
file's lines. The year's boundary is pinned from both sides through
`in_year`, the function the count uses: 1 January and 31 December 1851
are in the year, 31 December 1850 and 1 January 1852 are not.

## 6. Verdict

Reproduced: the roll holds 51 rows dated 1851, 49 ordinary and 2 fairs,
on 51 distinct days, as [claim 001] states; every row counted is dated
1851, and the file's own lines, month by month, leave none out. What to
check next is the note's figure, which this check does not read.

Each named mutation was applied by the probe (`tools/falsifiability_probe.py`),
and the check failed under each. The probe's matrix: the direct and the
wrapped runs pass; shift-year, drop-month, duplicate-entry and
relabel-kind CAUGHT. The count under each, computed by applying each
mutation to the roll's rows in memory and counting again: shift-year,
every date a year later, 0; drop-month, March 1851 removed, 47;
duplicate-entry, three rows of 1851 repeated, 54; relabel-kind, the
first fair of 1851 relabeled ordinary, 51 in all, split 50 and 1. The probe's reader's table for these four mutations, with its gaps and
their refuters' verdicts, is kept in `prompt_logs/008_tasks_3_and_4.md`;
the probe's reader's table of the check as first written and the two

Second pass: a reader without the first pass's reasoning, given the held

memory): 51 in all, 49 ordinary and 2 fairs, on 1851-05-15 and
1851-10-09. It agreed.
