## What was asked

The first step of the write-up's release (`paper/README.md`, *Releasing
a version*), as prompt 010 directed it: "the documentation sweep, split
among four readers by item as before, a refuter for each finding, and
the recount". The sweep is `governance/roles/documentation_sweep.md`,
its reading split as in round 003: items 1 and 2; 3 and 4; 5, 6 and 7;
8, 9 and 10. The recount is item 3 of the consistency sweep of the record
(`CHECK_METHODOLOGY.md` §7), over `CURRENT_STATE.md`, `FINDINGS.md`, and
`evidence_and_reasoning/research_result.md`, made by a fifth reader. Each
finding went to a refuter of its own (`governance/roles/refuter.md`). The
five readers and 35 refuters were subagents that wrote nothing.

## What was found

the entry of the round in progress, whose closing status was not yet
written.

of the 75 files on the prompt's list. Of the log they read the prompts of
entries 004 to 010, the last documentation sweep being that of prompt
003. The main thread compared the names they returned with the list and
had none left to read. The fifth reader read the three status pages, the
claims, and what their links and tokens name.

**The dates, item 5's first half.** The item reads a file's history, and
nothing is committed in this project before its end. The main thread
compared every file with the setup tool's output, kept outside the
project, in its place: 27 files have changed since setup and 31 are new,
each with the updated date of its change, and no file's date moved
without a change to the file.

30 to 35, are of other kinds.

## What follows

Eight decisions, brought for the researcher's ruling by number. Each
gives the options and their consequence; the assistant's recommendation
follows the options, labeled as its own. A ruling that changes a document
is applied in the round that records it.

1. **The form of a check** (findings 6, 14). In `CHECK_METHODOLOGY.md`,
   both places, "a written collation procedure, one dated execution per
   reading, for what the note states" would read "a written procedure
   with dated executions, a collation for what the note states or a
   derivation for a quantity derived from a count"; in
   `evidence_and_reasoning/checks/README.md`, "a written collation
   procedure for what the note states" would read "a written procedure, a
   collation for what the note states or a derivation for a quantity
   derived from a count". Options: (a) take both; (b) leave the value,
   [check 003] standing outside it. The assistant recommends (a).
2. **The mean** (findings 8, 33). `research_result.md` restates
   `FINDINGS.md`, which does not hold the mean. Options: (a) enter
   `| F003 | The roll's count for 1851 gives a mean of 4.25 market days a
   month | [claim 003] | DERIVED |`; (b) take the mean out of
   `research_result.md` and out of the write-up, where it does no work
   for the question, [claim 003] staying in the record; (c) leave both
   pages as they are. The assistant recommends (b).
3. **The weekdays** (finding 32). The note says the market was held on
   Tuesdays and that "the roll shows no day held on any other day of the
   week" [Note1851, p. 1]; a reader's count puts 49 of the roll's rows of
   1851 on Tuesdays and 2, the fairs, on Thursdays. No claim states this,
   so no tension row can be entered yet. Options: (a) add a task to plan
   001, before the review: a program that reads each row's weekday and
   the Tuesdays of 1851 with no row, a claim, and then whatever rows the
   researcher enters; (b) leave it to a later plan, the write-up saying
   nothing of it; (c) leave it. The assistant recommends (a): the
   write-up quotes that sentence, and its table lists the two fairs.
4. **The glossary** (findings 17, 18, 19, 20), in
   `evidence_and_reasoning/terminology.md`. Under *Names the sources

   say which; (c) none. The assistant recommends (a).
5. **The registry's key convention** (finding 25). "This project fixes
