# The market days of 1851: the roll against the note

*Created 6 September 2026; updated 6 September 2026.*
Released 6 September 2026

## Question

We ask whether the market roll of a fictional town supports the
count of market days for 1851 that a secondary note states for it. The
roll and the note are both synthetic files shipped with this
example, and nothing here is a claim about any real place; what we
establish is a fact about the two files.

## What the roll holds

The roll holds 51 rows dated in 1851, of which 49 are ordinary days
and 2 are fairs [claim 001]. We count every row dated in 1851 as one
market day, fairs included, which is what a row of the roll records,
and the rule of reading was fixed before the count was run; the roll,
being synthetic, was generated to the count it holds. The count is read
by a program whose count assertion fails, reporting a count other than
51, when every date's year is shifted forward and when one month's rows
are removed; the program's twelve monthly sums, computed from the rows
by a separate path, add to the same 51. The 49 ordinary rows fall on
Tuesdays and the two fairs, 15 May and 9 October, on Thursdays; the 51
rows fall on 51 distinct dates [claim 007]. The calendar year 1851 has
52 Tuesdays, and the roll holds no row for three of them, 4
February, 12 August, and 30 December [claim 007].

## What the note states

The note states that the roll records forty-eight market days for
1851, held on Tuesdays throughout the year with customary interruptions
and on no other day of the week [claim 002]. We collated the figure
against the note's first page and record what the note says, not
whether it is right.

## Comparison

The roll's count of market days exceeds the note's figure by three.
Duplicated rows do not explain the difference: no row of 1851 repeats
another's date and kind [claim 004], and since the 51 rows fall on 51
distinct dates, the excess is one of days, not of two entries on one
day. The difference survives that check; it is entered in the tension
ledger as a divergence between this project's count and the note, with
the caution that the note's counting convention is unknown. The note's statement that the
roll shows no day held on any day of the week but Tuesday diverges
from the roll as well, whose two fairs are Thursdays; this is
entered in the ledger with the same caution, since whether the note's
author counted fairs as days held is unknown.

## What is not settled

Why the two figures differ is not decided here. Two readings of the
note's figure are held open for a later plan, neither established: that
the note's author counted under a year beginning on 25 March, which the
record has not tested, and that the author counted ordinary Tuesdays
only, which gives 49, one more than the note states.
