# The market days of 1851: a synthetic roll and note compared

*Created 20 November 2026; updated 20 November 2026.*

## Question

We ask whether the market roll of a fictional town supports what a

## What the roll holds

The roll runs from 7 January 1851 to 28 December 1852; 1852 is outside

> days. The market was held on Tuesdays throughout the year, with the
> customary interruptions at the turn of the year and in the depth of
> winter, and the roll shows no day held on any other day of the week.

Its second page says that the roll for 1852 is fuller, and leaves a count
of that year to a later note.

## Comparison

On the number, the roll does not support the note. On the weekdays, it
supports the note for all 49 ordinary days, and departs from it on the
two fairs if a fair is a day held in the note's sense.

Our count of the roll, 51 in Table 1, exceeds the note's figure,
forty-eight on its first page, by three. No two rows of 1851 share a date, so the
difference does not come from days entered twice. Leaving the two fairs
out does not close it: the roll's ordinary days of 1851 are 49 in Table
2, one more than the note's forty-eight.

The note says the market was held on Tuesdays and that the roll shows no
day held on any other day of the week. The roll's 49 ordinary days fall
on Tuesdays and its 2 fairs on Thursdays in Table 2, the fairs of 15 May
and 9 October in Table 1. If the note counts a fair as a day held, those
two rows contradict what it says the roll shows; if it does not, what it
says holds of the other 49 rows.

The note gives the market's interruptions as customary, "at the turn of

4 February then falls in the depth of winter, and 12 August at neither of

if those two times were the only exceptions, and the note does not say

We do not settle why the two figures differ. The note does not say how
it counted, and the roll alone cannot say which count the note made, or
whether the note read the roll in a state other than the one we hold.
The note does not say whether a fair is a day held, or that the
interruptions it names are the only ones, so we do not say that the roll
contradicts it on the Thursdays or on 12 August: we say where the two
part.
