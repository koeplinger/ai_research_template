# The market days of 1851: a synthetic roll and note compared

*Created 20 November 2026; updated 20 November 2026.*
Released 20 November 2026
Plan: 001, task 5

## Question

We ask whether the market roll of a fictional town supports what a
fictional note states of its market days in 1851: their number, and the
days of the week they fell on. The roll and the note are synthetic files
made for this study, and we say nothing of any real market.

## What the roll holds

The roll runs from 7 January 1851 to 28 December 1852; 1852 is outside
our question. Each row gives a date, a kind, ordinary or fair, and a
remark, which only the fairs fill, with the fair's name. The roll states
no total for any year. It was written by a program held with it, and as
that program says, the roll records the days held: every Tuesday of the
two years and two fair days a year, less the Tuesdays the town's council
cancelled, with no reason given for a cancellation.

We count each row dated between 1 January and 31 December 1851 as a
market day held, fairs included. The roll treats a fair so, by the
program that wrote it; the note does not say whether it counts a fair.

**Table 1.** The roll's market days in 1851, the dates read from the roll
and the days counted from them.

|---|---|---:|
| January | 7, 14, 21, 28 | 4 |
| February | 11, 18, 25 | 3 |
| March | 4, 11, 18, 25 | 4 |
| April | 1, 8, 15, 22, 29 | 5 |
| May | 6, 13, 15 (spring fair), 20, 27 | 5 |
| June | 3, 10, 17, 24 | 4 |
| July | 1, 8, 15, 22, 29 | 5 |
| August | 5, 19, 26 | 3 |
| September | 2, 9, 16, 23, 30 | 5 |
| October | 7, 9 (autumn fair), 14, 21, 28 | 5 |
| November | 4, 11, 18, 25 | 4 |
| December | 2, 9, 16, 23 | 4 |
| **1851** | | **51** |

Our count of the roll's rows for 1851 is 51, 49 ordinary and 2 fairs, no
two on the same date. We make that count by a program of our own, which
checks it against the roll's own lines, month by month, and a second
count of the rows, by a separate run of our AI assistant given the roll
alone, gave the same figures.

We read each date on the Gregorian calendar, the calendar of the
international standard (ISO) year-month-day form in which the roll writes
its dates, nothing in the roll marking another. We work out each weekday
twice, by a software date library and by a standard weekday formula,
Zeller's congruence; the two agree on every day of 1851, which checks the
arithmetic and not the calendar.

**Table 2.** The roll's days of 1851 by weekday and kind, the kinds read
from the roll and the weekdays computed from its dates on the Gregorian
calendar.

| Weekday | Ordinary | Fair | All |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tuesday | 49 | 0 | 49 |
| Thursday | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| any other | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **1851** | **49** | **2** | **51** |

The roll holds a row on 49 of the 52 Tuesdays that 1851 has on the
Gregorian calendar, and none on 4 February, 12 August, or 30 December.

## What the note states

The note runs to two pages. Its first page reads, whole:

> The town's market roll for the year 1851 records forty-eight market
> days. The market was held on Tuesdays throughout the year, with the
> customary interruptions at the turn of the year and in the depth of
> winter, and the roll shows no day held on any other day of the week.

Its second page says that the roll for 1852 is fuller, and leaves a count
of that year to a later note.

## Comparison

On the number, the roll does not support the note. On the weekdays, it
supports the note for all 49 ordinary days, and departs from it on the
two fairs if a fair is a day held in the note's sense.

Our count of the roll, 51 in Table 1, exceeds the note's figure,
forty-eight on its first page, by three. No two rows of 1851 share a date, so the
difference does not come from days entered twice. Leaving the two fairs
out does not close it: the roll's ordinary days of 1851 are 49 in Table
2, one more than the note's forty-eight.

The note says the market was held on Tuesdays and that the roll shows no
day held on any other day of the week. The roll's 49 ordinary days fall
on Tuesdays and its 2 fairs on Thursdays in Table 2, the fairs of 15 May
and 9 October in Table 1. If the note counts a fair as a day held, those
two rows contradict what it says the roll shows; if it does not, what it
says holds of the other 49 rows.

The note gives the market's interruptions as customary, "at the turn of
the year and in the depth of winter", and gives no dates for them. Of
the roll's three Tuesdays without a row, 30 December falls at the turn of
the year. The roll names its fair of 15 May a spring fair and its fair of
9 October an autumn fair, so we read its year as having northern seasons:
4 February then falls in the depth of winter, and 12 August at neither of
the two times the note names. The note's "throughout the year" reads as
if those two times were the only exceptions, and the note does not say
that they are.

## What is not settled

We do not settle why the two figures differ. The note does not say how
it counted, and the roll alone cannot say which count the note made, or
whether the note read the roll in a state other than the one we hold.
The note does not say whether a fair is a day held, or that the
interruptions it names are the only ones, so we do not say that the roll
contradicts it on the Thursdays or on 12 August: we say where the two
part.
