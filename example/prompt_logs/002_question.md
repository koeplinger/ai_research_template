--- PROMPT START ---
The question: does the market roll, a synthetic file of the market days
of a fictional town in 1851 and 1852 made for this project, support the
count of market days for 1851 that a fictional note made beside it
states? A count of the roll's rows for 1851, by a check that would fail
were the count otherwise, set against the note's figure as collated
against the note itself, answers it. A count reported from memory, or
the note's figure quoted without collation, does not.

The thesis is one hypothesis, H-A: the roll and the note disagree on
1851. The roll's count of market days held in 1851 differs from the
count the note states for that year. What would count against it: a
count of the roll's 1851 rows, by a check that would fail were it
otherwise, equal to the note's figure as collated against the note.

Lines of work: count the roll's 1851 rows and collate the note's figure;
where the two differ, decide what the roll itself can decide about the
difference, and set aside what it cannot. Not pursued: the count for
1852, since the question is the note's figure for 1851.

Write the research statement, the keystone, and the front page's
paragraph on the question. Nothing else yet.
--- PROMPT END ---
